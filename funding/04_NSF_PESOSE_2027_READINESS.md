# NSF PESOSE Track 1 readiness plan

**Target:** March 2, 2027.

**Status:** preparation only. Witness is not represented as a mature open-source ecosystem, and this document does not claim eligibility or application readiness.

## Why PESOSE may fit later

NSF PESOSE Track 1 supports scoping and planning for sustainable, secure open-source ecosystems: governance, contributor and user community discovery, licensing, fundraising, administration, and milestones. Witness’s long-term goal — an open evidence and provenance layer that can be used across scientific and public-interest domains — may fit if the project can show a robust open product, real demand, and an external community.

## What must be true by the application decision

| Requirement | Current state | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defined open product | Early Witness prototype and documentation | Protocol v1, conformance fixtures, supported producer path, release process. |
| Societal need and user demand | Mission and founder hypothesis | Interviews, pilot partner, letters that describe a real problem in the partner’s words. |
| Distributed developer community | No demonstrated outside contributor base | At least several outside contributors or review participants with defined roles and transparent governance. |
| User community | No adoption demonstrated | One or more scoped pilots, use feedback, and documented limits. |
| Governance and licensing | Published founding governance and licensing policy | Multi-stakeholder advisory or governing structure appropriate to the actual community. |
| Security and quality controls | Local tests, signing prototype, public boundaries | Threat model, release/checkpoint process, CI, vulnerability response, and external security review plan. |
| Sustainable organization | NextEleven LLC exists; applicant structure not verified | Confirm eligible lead organization, UEI/SAM readiness, PI eligibility, financial administration, and conflict policy. |
| Milestones and evaluation | Early roadmap and Proof B plan | Measurable Track 1 workplan tied to community discovery and decision points. |

## Backward plan

### September–November 2026

- Complete external reviewer outreach and record responses with consent.
- Identify a domain collaborator and negotiate a small Proof B scope.
- Hold at least three user-discovery interviews with research organizations.
- Build the Proof B protocol, source-rights inventory, and budget.
- Decide whether NextEleven or a partner organization should eventually lead.

### December 2026–January 2027

- Execute or begin the independently designed Proof B.
- Publish a reproducible package and an external reproduction attempt.
- Establish a small advisory circle with disclosed conflicts and no implied endorsement.
- Draft governance, contributor onboarding, release, and security procedures based on actual use.

### February 2027

- Compare the completed evidence to the PESOSE solicitation.
- Verify applicant organization and PI eligibility directly with NSF guidance.
- Obtain only truthful collaboration letters from real prospective users or contributors.
- Decide whether to apply, defer, or seek a university/nonprofit lead.

### March 2, 2027

Submit only if the evidence supports the proposal. A deferral is preferable to inflating current maturity.

## Track 1 concept, if earned

A one-year Track 1 project could scope a secure open-source evidence-record ecosystem across two concrete producer communities, establish governance and contributor processes, define conformance and security requirements, test user needs, and publish a sustainability plan. It should not promise a universal truth system or broad deployment before there is an evidence base for either.

# Current evidence, claim boundaries, and funding risks

## Claims supportable today

- Witness and Humanity Grid repositories are public and have named software licenses, citation metadata, contribution provenance rules, and public reviewer materials.
- EXP-001 Proof A is frozen in annotated `proof-a-v1` tags in both repositories.
- The Humanity Grid proof-package verifier checks 43 listed artifacts, frozen internal-consensus values, and a Witness export containing 12 Observed, two Inferred, and one Generated record.
- Witness has a documented local review path and core automated tests.
- Three external organizations have received bounded requests for critique or referral. A sent request is not a response, partnership, endorsement, or review.

## Claims not supportable today

- Independent scientific validation of Proof A.
- A drug candidate, biological activity, docking accuracy, clinical value, or scientific discovery.
- Independent execution, external custody, source identity, or producer identity for the frozen computational artifacts.
- Customer demand, adoption, revenue, product-market fit, or a viable business model.
- A deployed public volunteer-computing network.
- A funded grant, a formal collaboration, or external endorsement.
- Readiness for consequential scientific, clinical, regulatory, or public-policy decisions.

## Principal funding risks and mitigation

| Risk | Why it matters | Current mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overclaiming scientific meaning | A funder may mistake infrastructure verification for a scientific result. | Lead with the Proof A boundary and fund an independent Proof B. |
| No named user | Grants and funders need a concrete problem owner. | Conduct user discovery and recruit a domain collaborator before large applications. |
| Founder-only development | Reviewers will question scientific, security, and governance independence. | Fund external domain and reproducibility roles; preserve their criticism. |
| Broad vision / unclear wedge | “Public witness for reality” is too large to evaluate as a first grant. | Sell one use case: inspectable records for AI-assisted computational research. |
| Open-source defensibility | An open protocol alone may be copied. | Build value in verification, conformance, hosted operation, integration, support, and trusted implementation practice. |
| Rights and entity ambiguity | Funder due diligence will ask who can grant rights and receive funds. | Confirm ownership, contractor terms, and applicant eligibility before a formal application. |
| Insufficient independent evidence | Proof A is founder-created. | Obtain and publish review, reproduction, and a domain-defined Proof B. |

## The funding narrative to use

“AI-assisted research needs a record that makes it possible to distinguish what was measured, what was inferred, and what was generated. Proof A shows a local, reproducible record path. Funding will test that infrastructure through an independently defined and reviewed Proof B.”

## The funding narrative to avoid

- “We have solved truth.”
- “Our AI discovered a drug candidate.”
- “The system has been independently validated.”
- “Humanity Grid is an operating global compute network.”
- “Witness prevents misinformation.”
- “Outside reviewers or organizations endorse the project.”
